How I teach

Learn to make progress with an unfamiliar problem.

Being ahead in the school syllabus does not always make challenge questions straightforward. Often the key is to draw a useful diagram, test a small case or spot a simple structure before reaching for more advanced mathematics.

I use the student's attempts to guide each lesson, with carefully judged hints, discussion of different approaches and targeted work between sessions. Preparation can focus on a particular competition or on developing problem-solving skills over a longer period.

More about my teaching
  • Number theory, algebra and geometryDivisibility, remainders, factorisation, inequalities and diagrams that reveal what matters.
  • Counting and logical reasoningOrganise cases, use symmetry and parity, and recognise when a simple example can guide the argument.
  • Accuracy and competition techniqueChoose questions sensibly, use time well and review both errors and unnecessarily long methods.
  • Writing a convincing proofFor BMO preparation, move from noticing why something might be true to explaining clearly why it must be true.

SMC — Senior Mathematical Challenge

Develop the fluency and judgement needed for demanding challenge questions, with preparation for BMO proof problems where appropriate.

90 minutes · 25 questions · 125 marks

Year 13 and below in England, Wales and overseas; S6 and below in Scotland; Year 14 and below in Northern Ireland. Candidates must be full-time students at secondary school or FE college. Official competition information

SMC format from 2026: 22 multiple-choice questions and three questions with a three-digit answer. The 2023–2025 papers below use the earlier multiple-choice format. See the current format.

BMO — British Mathematical Olympiad

BMO preparation focuses on proof. Lessons develop ways to explore a problem, identify a promising argument and turn it into a complete written solution, with detailed discussion of gaps and unjustified steps.
